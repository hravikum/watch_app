{# --- Section: Normalization --- #}

Normalization

Raw signal values live on wildly different scales — mention_count might reach 50 while source_diversity tops out around 5 and first_seen_recency is bounded at 1.0. Combining them with fixed weights only makes sense if they're comparable.

Each signal is z-score normalized across the current maker population before composition: z = (raw − mean) / stddev. After normalization every signal has mean 0 and stddev 1, so a weight of 0.6 means “roughly six tenths of a standard deviation” regardless of the signal's raw scale.

The independence flag is the exception — it's a 0/1 gate, not a continuous signal, so z-scoring it would break its multiplier semantics. It's passed through unchanged.

First-surfaced counts the makers whose first-ever mention came from this source — a measure of how much genuine discovery work it does versus re-covering what's already been seen elsewhere.
